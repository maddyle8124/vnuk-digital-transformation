2. Transformation Domain Identification

Purpose. Domain identification defines the right end-to-end unit of transformation. The domain must be large enough to create meaningful value, yet focused enough to manage.

2.1 What is a transformation domain?

A transformation domain is an end-to-end set of related activities and decisions that begins with a trigger and produces a meaningful, measurable outcome for a beneficiary. A domain crosses functional boundaries and can contain several related digital solutions and use cases.

A useful domain is:

  • Large enough: it can create material value.
  • Focused enough: its scope, dependencies, and ownership are manageable.
  • Cross-functional: it includes important handoffs and decisions across organizational units.
  • Outcome-defined: completion and success can be expressed through business KPIs.
  • Reusable: it can support a coherent portfolio of related use cases and shared capabilities.

A domain is not simply a department, system, technology, or isolated use case. Examples include customer onboarding, order fulfillment, fraud management, student recruitment, and equipment maintenance.

2.2 Domain Boundary Canvas

Define each candidate domain consistently using six elements:

  1. Beneficiary: Who receives value?
  2. Start event: What triggers the domain?
  3. End outcome: What marks successful completion?
  4. Core activities: Which activities, decisions, and handoffs belong inside?
  5. Exclusions: What is deliberately outside the domain?
  6. Measures: Which outcome, operational, and adoption indicators will be used?

Clear boundaries prevent teams from defining a scope that is either a tiny feature or an unmanageable enterprise-wide transformation.

2.3 Build the organization’s domain portfolio

Map outcomes rather than reproducing the organization chart. A portfolio might include winning customers, onboarding customers, delivering products, serving and retaining customers, managing the workforce, and managing risk. The domain names should describe an action and an outcome rather than a functional label such as “IT,” “Marketing,” or “Operations.”

Teams should normally identify six to ten domains, then shortlist three candidates for deeper assessment.

2.4 Assess current-state capability by candidate domain

Rewired presents six mutually reinforcing capabilities. For each candidate domain, assess:

  • Roadmap: clarity of priorities, value ownership, and leadership alignment.
  • Talent: availability of business, product, data, technology, and change skills.
  • Operating model: cross-functional teamwork, decision rights, funding, and accountability.
  • Technology: modularity, integration, security, reliability, and scalable delivery.
  • Data: trusted, accessible, reusable data and clear ownership.
  • Adoption and scaling: changed behavior, process reinforcement, replication, and measurable impact.

Capability should be rated relative to the candidate domain, not as a generic enterprise score. The same organization may be ready in one domain and constrained in another.

2.5 Business-domain Mapping activity

List major value streams, combine related activities into outcome-producing domains, name each domain with an action and outcome, and shortlist three candidates. For each candidate, complete a Domain Boundary Canvas and note the most important capability gap.
