1. Current-state Analysis

Purpose. Current-state analysis establishes what is happening now, why performance problems persist, and which capability constraints must be addressed before selecting a transformation domain. Digital diagnosis is not a technology list, a generic maturity score, or an early solution proposal.

1.1 What digital diagnosis is

A useful diagnosis connects three managerial actions:

  • Observe: identify what customers, employees, partners, and operations experience.
  • Explain: examine why symptoms persist across processes, decisions, data, technology, roles, governance, skills, and adoption.
  • Focus: determine where coordinated change could create measurable business value.

Rewired emphasizes that digital value comes from mutually reinforcing enterprise capabilities. Therefore, diagnosis should investigate the organizational system around a problem, rather than assuming that a new tool will solve it.

1.2 Build a current-state evidence base

Diagnosis should triangulate evidence from multiple sources. A single interview, complaint, or KPI is rarely sufficient to establish a cause.

  • Performance evidence: cycle time, cost, error rate, conversion, service level, throughput, abandonment, and risk events.
  • Customer and user evidence: interviews, observations, complaints, contact reasons, workarounds, and channel abandonment.
  • Process evidence: queues, handoffs, rework, exceptions, approvals, control points, and process variation.
  • Capability evidence: ownership, decision rights, skills, data quality, architecture, integration, incentives, and adoption.

For each item, record the source, date, scope, baseline, reliability, and owner. Separate observed facts from interpretations and assumptions.

1.3 Symptoms, causes, gaps, and impact

A symptom is the visible problem, such as slow application approval. An immediate cause may be manual checks and repeated handoffs. A system cause may be inconsistent rules or duplicated data. A capability gap may be the absence of end-to-end ownership or weak data integration. The business impact may include abandonment, cost, delayed revenue, or reduced trust.

Every claimed cause requires evidence. If evidence is not yet available, label the cause as a hypothesis and identify what evidence would confirm or reject it.

1.4 Map the current journey and handoffs

A journey map becomes diagnostic when it shows the beneficiary, start event, end outcome, customer steps, front-office work, back-office activities, decisions, data sources, systems, handoffs, waiting time, and ownership. Read the map horizontally to follow the beneficiary journey and vertically to examine dependencies between customer actions, operational work, and technology.

Use four diagnostic questions:

  1. Delay: Where does work wait? Examine queues, approvals, missing information, and unavailable capacity.
  2. Rework: Where is work repeated? Examine repeated entry, corrections, duplicate checks, and repeated contact.
  3. Decision friction: Where is judgment blocked? Examine unclear rules, authority, data, or escalation paths.
  4. Customer friction: Where does the journey break? Examine extra effort, uncertainty, channel breaks, and unclear status.

For every marker on the map, identify supporting evidence, an accountable owner, and the KPI affected.

1.5 Current-state Evidence Map activity

Select one visible problem. List available evidence, separate symptoms from causes, identify missing evidence, and share one insight. The required output is one evidence map and one explicit evidence gap. Do not propose a solution during this activity.
