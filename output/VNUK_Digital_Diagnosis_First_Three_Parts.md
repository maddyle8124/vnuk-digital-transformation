## 1. Current-state analysis

### CSE: existing academic support, uneven access and process clarity

The interviews document functioning academic support alongside inconsistent student experiences. They do not support a blanket claim that lecturers are unhelpful, career support does not exist or practical teaching is absent.

| Area | Evidence from supplied interviews | Supported conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Academic support | One student reports waiting 4–5 days for an advising email. Others describe helpful postgraduate advice, support outside class and weekly thesis meetings. [I2, I3, I4] | Support exists, but timeliness and access vary. The 4–5 days is one recalled case, not an average. |
| Course registration | Two students describe confusing registration and system lag. One reports mixed programme listings and uncertainty about whether registration succeeded. [I1, I4] | Navigation and confirmation are documented friction points. Frequency and technical causes are unmeasured. |
| Thesis guidance | Some students describe unclear rules or needing to ask several people. Others report useful topic discussions or regular supervision. Several had only just started the process. [I2–I5] | Guidance varies by student and stage. Early-stage uncertainty must not be treated as evidence of failure across the entire thesis process. |
| Graduation administration | One alumnus reports not receiving a graduation-registration email and needing faculty intervention. Two other accounts describe repeatedly checking deadlines and fragmented requirements. [I3, I5] | The files support information and coordination problems. They do not establish an error rate or actual delay in graduation. |
| Practical learning and career preparation | Some respondents describe insufficient workplace preparation. Others describe realistic web-development teaching, useful industry practitioners and a balance of theory and practice. [I2–I5] | Perceived relevance differs across courses and students. Institution-wide claims of “theory only” would misrepresent the evidence. |
| Computing resources | Students report old computers; an alumnus describes inadequate GPU access and help with GPU costs from a lecturer. [I1, I3, I4] | Resource adequacy needs examination. Hardware specifications, inventory and funding arrangements are not verified. |
| Organisation and coordination | Faculty notes describe leadership, academic administration and lecturers, with a student-affairs focal point and issues routed between Student Affairs and the faculty. [I1, p.5] | A coordination structure exists. The notes also describe a small unit and limited activity beyond core teaching/research. Spare capacity is unknown. |

**Interpretation:** the interviews point to uneven access to guidance and fragmented administrative coordination, while also showing valuable academic support. A plausible cause is dependence on individual contacts, student initiative and separate communications. This remains a process hypothesis, not proof that governance is the sole cause.

### BMS: laboratory conditions and practical-learning constraints

The full report contains survey charts for eight BMS respondents. The counts below have been checked against Figures 1–7. They describe this sample, not department-wide prevalence or newly measured current rates. [B3 §3.1.2]

**Correction to the old report:** Figure 1 shows satisfaction ratings of 2 (one respondent), 3 (three), 4 (two) and 5 (two), with no rating of 1. The weighted mean is (2×1 + 3×3 + 4×2 + 5×2)/8 = **3.625/5**, not approximately 2/5 as the narrative and earlier summary state. Half the respondents rate facilities 4 or 5. Overall facility satisfaction and specific heat-related problems can coexist.

| Finding | Reported result | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Laboratory satisfaction | **3.625/5**, calculated from 8 charted ratings | Mixed-to-positive overall ratings, alongside specific complaints. Corrects the report narrative. |
| Heat-related fatigue, irritability or reduced productivity | 7/8 students, 87.5% | Perceived effect of laboratory conditions, not measured productivity loss. |
| Wanting to finish experiments quickly to leave | 5/8, 62.5% | Reported response to laboratory discomfort. |
| Insufficient practical laboratory time | 4/8, 50% | An access or capacity issue to investigate; cause not established. |
| Heavy Molecular Biology/Chemistry workload | 6/8, 75% | Perceived academic workload. |
| Difficulty remembering specialist English terminology | 2/8, 25% | A specific reported learning-support need. |
| Selecting lecturers/advisors as a stress-coping route | 0/8, 0% | None selected this response. It does not prove the absence of advising services. |

The heat, academic-pain, preference, motivation and coping charts have category counts summing above eight, so their categories overlap and must not be added as mutually exclusive shares.

Additional evidence: **8/8** select a preference for hands-on experimentation, **5/8** rate stress at 7 or 8 out of 10, and **7/8** select pressure not to waste family tuition as a motivation. These are preferences and self-reports, not proof of fixed learning types, a clinical condition or a causal effect of teaching methods. Figure 3 has **0/8** selections for unsuitable teaching methods and **0/8** for insufficient/hard-to-access reference materials; this limits claims that the survey demonstrates those particular problems. [B3, Figures 3–6]

**Interpretation:** BMS evidence identifies laboratory comfort, practical-learning time and academic workload as concerns. The full report also describes cumbersome equipment borrowing and limited technical guidance during some afternoon lab hours. These are report-level qualitative findings without a linked faculty transcript or staffing log. Budget or administrative rules are possible explanations raised by the separate interview protocol, not verified causes. [B3 §§3.1.1.1, 3.1.2.1; B2]

## 2. Problem-domain identification

The following eight domains describe activities with a start and an outcome. They are not ranked. Technology roles indicate what could be investigated, not selected solutions.

| Domain and boundary | CSE evidence | BMS evidence | Potential technology role and limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Course planning and registration:** planning begins to valid course selection confirmed | Confusing listings, lag and uncertain confirmation. [I1, I4] | No direct evidence supplied | Guidance, rule visibility and confirmation support; require accurate rules and system access. |
| **Thesis registration and supervision:** topic selection to completion of agreed supervision milestones | Mixed clarity and support, with both helpful supervision and “self-navigation” accounts. [I2–I5] | No completed interview evidence supplied | Topic guidance, milestone/status visibility and coordination; cannot substitute for supervisor time. |
| **Graduation clearance:** eligibility review to completed application and resolved exceptions | Missed registration notice and fragmented deadline/document information. [I3, I5] | No direct evidence supplied | Requirement visibility and case tracking; authoritative records and accountable reviewers remain necessary. |
| **Academic advising and enquiry resolution:** question raised to answer or completed referral | Delayed response in one account, positive guidance in others. [I2–I5] | Survey coping response does not establish service performance | Routing, information access and request tracking; actual demand and response capacity must be measured. |
| **Practical-learning resource access:** resource need to completed practical session | Reports of old computers and constrained computing access. [I1, I3, I4] | 4/8 report insufficient practical time. [B3] | Availability and booking information; scheduling cannot create equipment or supervised capacity. |
| **Laboratory condition and maintenance management:** condition issue identified to verified restoration | Computer adequacy complaints; no maintenance records | 7/8 report heat-related difficulties and mean satisfaction 3.625/5. [B3] | Fault reporting and maintenance visibility; cooling and hardware constraints can require physical investment. |
| **Learning support:** difficulty identified to support delivered and understanding reassessed | Contrasting accounts of explanation, practice and self-study. [I1–I5] | 6/8 report heavy workload; 2/8 terminology difficulty. [B3] | Approved learning resources and formative practice; pedagogical causes require academic assessment. |
| **Career and research opportunity access:** need identified to guidance, application or referral completed | Mixed career advice and one missed research-funding opportunity. [I2–I5] | Career questions exist in the protocol but no answers. [B2] | Opportunity information and referral visibility; cannot establish employer partnerships or funding availability. |

The same resource complaint can inform both access and maintenance, but it must not be counted twice as independent evidence of value. Cross-department applicability remains a question to test where only one department has evidence.

### Capability conclusion

**CSE:** academic and digital capabilities exist, but the evidence does not demonstrate consistent service delivery or available capacity for a new intervention. The assessment must distinguish individual expertise from reliable departmental processes.

**BMS:** the full report adds evidence of laboratory access friction, reported gaps in technical guidance, fragmented feedback and limited measurement. It still does not establish staff availability, operational ownership, an approved budget or implementation capacity. Its maturity labels are the research team’s judgments, not an independently verified capability score. The proposed Lab Portal, digital inventory, 16:00–19:00 Open Lab hours and CX Council must not be counted as existing capabilities.

**Across VNUK:** the current evidence supports diagnosis of specific historical problems. It does not yet justify a confirmed high-feasibility rating, institution-wide causal claim or estimated financial return.
