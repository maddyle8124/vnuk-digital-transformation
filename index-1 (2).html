3. Transformation Domain Prioritization

Purpose. Prioritization compares candidate domains transparently and selects one defensible focus for the transformation strategy and roadmap developed in Session 4.

3.1 Prioritization criteria

The illustrative weighting in the session is:

  • Value potential, 30%: revenue, cost, risk, experience, productivity, resilience, and strategic value.
  • Feasibility, 25%: data, process, skills, technology, integration, time, and organizational readiness.
  • Strategic relevance, 20%: differentiation, leadership priority, customer promise, and business-model importance.
  • Urgency, 15%: market, regulatory, competitive, customer, or operational pressure.
  • Learning value, 10%: reusable capability, evidence, data products, platforms, and lessons for later domains.

The weights are not universal. Teams should state and justify their weighting assumptions and apply the same definitions to all candidates.

3.2 Value–feasibility matrix

Plot candidate domains on a portfolio view. High-value domains that are feasible enough may be prioritized. High-value domains with major capability gaps may require foundation work before full transformation. Lower-value or less urgent domains may be monitored. The position of each domain must be supported by evidence rather than intuition.

3.3 Candidate-domain comparison

Scoring structures debate but does not replace judgment. Compare value, feasibility, strategic fit, urgency, learning value, and risk. Show the strongest rejected alternative and explain the trade-off.

Apply five decision checks:

  • Causal logic: Is there a credible connection between current problems, causes, capability gaps, and expected value?
  • Boundary quality: Is the domain end-to-end, outcome-defined, and manageable?
  • Dependency load: How much foundation, integration, policy, or external coordination is required?
  • Adoption reality: Will users, managers, partners, and control functions change behavior?
  • Evidence access: Can the team establish a baseline and obtain evidence during the project?

3.4 Integrated priority-domain workshop

Combine current-state evidence, domain mapping, capability assessment, and prioritization. Map six to ten domains, shortlist three, rate capability gaps for each candidate, compare value and feasibility, then choose and defend one priority domain. Keep the work focused on diagnosis and domain selection, not detailed solution design.

3.5 Initial digital transformation diagnosis

The three-minute executive diagnosis should cover: strategic pressure; current-state problem; supporting evidence; root causes and capability gaps; priority domain and comparison with alternatives; and the next question Session 4 should investigate.

The Progress Report 1 evidence package includes the current-state problem and evidence, current journey or process map, organization domain map, three candidate domain canvases, capability-gap assessment, value–feasibility matrix, priority-domain recommendation, and evidence gaps with next actions.

Textbook connection. Rewired recommends choosing a transformation “bite size” that is large enough to create meaningful value but focused enough to mobilize leadership, teams, technology, and data around an end-to-end outcome. Domain prioritization creates the concentration needed for a coherent roadmap.
